Kanch-Net
Voice-first production aggregation for Firozabad glass artisans
Primary theme: Heritage & Culture
Product stage: SIH 2026 software-only prototype
Status: Final corrected scope — implementation ready
Prepared on: 22 August 2026
Primary user: Home-based glass-artisan households that own their finished goods
Deployment partner: Local cooperative, producer collective or trusted aggregator
Core promise  Kanch-Net helps many small artisan households behave like one reliable supplier: compatible batches are pooled into a buyer-ready lot, traced through collection and quality checks, and settled transparently.
Product boundary  The SIH prototype does not hold money, operate real escrow, issue loans, authenticate Aadhaar, verify GST through a private API or guarantee buyer orders. Payments, identity and material financing are simulated as auditable workflows for later execution through authorised partners.
Executive summary
Firozabad's home-based glass artisans often produce quantities too small for wholesale buyers. Even when a household owns its material and finished goods, it may still depend on a local middleman for market access. Kanch-Net records household inventory by voice, matches only compatible products, combines them into traceable bulk lots and coordinates collection, quality confirmation and transparent settlement.
The corrected MVP removes the earlier cash-flow mistake: a 20–30% buyer booking amount cannot fund 100% immediate artisan payment. Real settlement therefore follows accepted milestones through a licensed payment partner, while the prototype demonstrates the money logic using a mock ledger. A Lot Specification Card and batch-level quality traceability are mandatory, because quantity without consistency has no value to a wholesale buyer.

SIH form-ready summary
Problem statement: Home-based Firozabad glass artisans who own their finished goods remain dependent on middlemen because their individual output is too small and inconsistent for direct wholesale orders, and no low-literacy system combines compatible household batches into traceable, buyer-ready lots.
Short solution: Kanch-Net is a voice-first cooperative operating system that records artisan inventory in local terminology, matches it against a buyer's quality specification, pools compatible batches and tracks collection, quality and settlement through a transparent ledger. It does not replace physical quality control or licensed payment partners; it gives the cooperative the software needed to coordinate them.
1. Product context
1.1 Ground-level problem
One household may produce only a small batch, while a buyer wants a large, standardised lot.
The buyer cannot call, inspect and settle with dozens of households separately.
The artisan may not know the buyer's exact size, colour, finish, packaging and delivery requirements.
A low-literacy household cannot be expected to maintain a complex catalogue or spreadsheet.
When quality, quantity and payment are not traceable, both sides rely on a trusted middleman.
1.2 Heritage justification
Firozabad Glass is a registered geographical indication, valid through February 2029, and glass products are an official Firozabad ODOP category. The product is therefore positioned as economic infrastructure for a living craft, not as a generic marketplace with a heritage label.
Pitch insight  A craft survives only when the people carrying the skill can earn reliably from it.

1.4 Example
A wholesale buyer needs 10,000 red bangles of one size and finish. No single household can supply that quantity. Twenty households record their available batches by voice. Kanch-Net excludes incompatible colours or sizes, groups the matching batches, assigns batch IDs and prepares one collection plan. The cooperative checks accepted quantity and quality before the payment partner settles each accepted batch.

3. Corrected operating model
3.1 Order-first flow
The buyer or cooperative creates a Lot Specification Card.
Artisans record available or producible batches using voice or assisted entry.
The matching engine accepts only specification-compatible batches.
The cooperative confirms the proposed lot and collection cluster.
The collector verifies household quantity and basic visible quality, then assigns a batch status.
At consolidation, the cooperative records final accepted, rejected and damaged quantity.
The settlement simulator allocates buyer payment, material deduction and platform/co-op fee per accepted batch.
In production, a licensed payment partner—not Kanch-Net—executes settlement.

3.3 Batch traceability
Every household submission receives a batch ID and QR code.
The batch stores claimed quantity, collection quantity, accepted quantity and rejection reason.
Photographs are supporting evidence, not proof of legal ownership or perfect quality.
The buyer sees aggregated lot data; household personal details remain hidden unless required for settlement.
A rejected batch remains attributable so the cooperative can resolve the issue fairly.

3.5 Category 3 material allocation
The prototype may calculate a material requirement against a confirmed order, but it must not present this as an app-issued loan. A future cooperative may procure material centrally and allocate it for an already-specified order, subject to legal, accounting and working-capital review. The platform records quantity, cost, consent and deduction; a real partner provides the funds and bears the financial responsibility.

5.4 Buyer and settlement
Buyer sees the lot specification, aggregate accepted quantity and status.
The SIH demo simulates booking and balance events.
The ledger calculates household payouts using accepted quantities.
Every deduction is visible; the platform never silently changes the rate.

6.2 Quality states
Declared: artisan-stated quantity and specification

6.4 Fairness requirements
A rejected batch must show the reason to the artisan or facilitator.
Coordinator overrides must record the actor, time and explanation.
The algorithm must not rank households using caste, religion, gender or unrelated identity fields.
When supply exceeds order quantity, the MVP shows a fair-rotation recommendation; the coordinator confirms it.
7. Trust, identity and settlement boundaries
7.1 Artisan onboarding
Use ordinary phone OTP for the prototype.
Use community vouching as a social trust signal, not proof of legal identity.
Do not store full Aadhaar numbers or request Aadhaar OTP.
Future offline Aadhaar verification requires compliant OVSE processes and consent.
7.2 Buyer onboarding
Collect organisation/contact details and GSTIN when relevant.
Label GST status as self-declared, manually reviewed or future verified.
Do not claim an open GST verification API unless the team has documented access.
Use trial-order limits and booking commitment as future trust mechanisms.
7.3 Payment boundary
Mandatory wording  Kanch-Net provides order, quality and settlement instructions. It does not hold buyer funds in the SIH prototype. Production payments must be executed by a bank or authorised payment partner under an approved commercial arrangement.
7.4 Transparent ledger
For each household: accepted quantity × agreed unit price = gross value. Then show material cost, cooperative/service fee and other expressly authorised deductions. Net payout is never calculated from declared or merely collected quantity.

11.1 Kill criteria
No buyer confirms that multi-household aggregation solves a real purchase problem.
The team cannot define one product specification precisely enough to match batches.
The payment story still relies on a booking amount that is smaller than promised payouts.
The cooperative/field partner cannot perform physical collection and QC.
The team cannot protect household identities from unnecessary buyer access.

12. Pilot and roadmap
Phase 0 — SIH prototype
Synthetic buyers and households, one product specification, voice confirmation, deterministic matching, batch QR/QC and simulated settlement.
Phase 1 — Field validation
Interview 10–20 artisans and at least three buyers. Replace placeholder terminology, quantities, rates and tolerances with field-confirmed data.
Phase 2 — One-cooperative shadow pilot
Run the software beside the existing process without moving money. Measure logging time, match accuracy, rejected quantity and coordinator effort.
Phase 3 — Authorised transaction pilot
Add a licensed payment partner and written buyer/cooperative terms. Complete legal, accounting, privacy, grievance and material-allocation review before real settlement.
Phase 4 — Replication
Adapt the schema and terminology for another craft only after the Firozabad workflow is proven. Do not assume the same ownership and quality structure applies everywhere.
13. Jury-ready positioning
13.1 Novelty
Kanch-Net is not another artisan catalogue. Its product is an operational compiler: it converts many low-literacy household batches into one specification-compliant, traceable lot, while keeping each household's quality and settlement visible.
13.2 Unfair advantage
The defensible layer is the local labour taxonomy, product vocabulary, quality rules and production history—not speech recognition or the map.
13.3 One-line pitch
Pitch  Kanch-Net lets dozens of Firozabad glass-artisan households act like one reliable wholesale supplier—using voice inventory, specification matching, batch-level quality and transparent settlement.
